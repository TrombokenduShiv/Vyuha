VYUHA 2.0
AGENCY INTEGRITY LAYER FOR COERCION-DRIVEN DIGITAL PAYMENT FRAUD
30-Page Product Requirements Document (PRD) + Technical Architecture Specification

System thesis: local agency sensing + institutional graph intelligence + safety-constrained adaptive intervention.


Prepared for implementation planning, hackathon execution, technical due diligence, mentor review and jury Q&A. This document deliberately separates evidence-backed platform capabilities from proposed Vyuha targets and hypotheses.

DOCUMENT GOVERNANCE
PRD Control, Evidence Semantics and Decision Principles
This PRD is a controlled engineering specification rather than a marketing deck. The submitted concept describes Vyuha as an on-device Edge-AI behavioral defense SDK using a sub-50 ms MoE triage, an ST-GNN, a contextual bandit and a trusted-node intervention. The present specification preserves that deep-tech intent while correcting deployment assumptions, moving institution-scale graph learning to the institutional plane, removing unsupported zero-false-positive/zero-battery/compliance guarantees, and defining measurable acceptance criteria.


Design principles
• Agency before anomaly: the system protects the quality of the user decision, not merely whether a transaction looks statistically unusual.
• Local first: sensitive context is interpreted on-device whenever possible. The core edge decision must continue without a cloud inference dependency.
• Graph intelligence where graph data exists: mule-ring inference belongs inside participating financial institutions or authorised fraud-intelligence infrastructure, not on an isolated handset.
• Friction is a sensor: interventions are chosen not only to warn but to obtain safe information and deliberately break the scammer’s synchronous control loop.
• Uncertainty is a first-class output: when evidence is weak or out-of-distribution, the system should abstain from aggressive action and choose proportionate friction.
• No magic integration claims: the production path assumes consented integration inside a participating UPI/PSP/bank application and bank-side risk services; a generic payment gateway cannot control every UPI authorization flow.
• Research claims become product claims only after an evaluation gate defined in this PRD.


PRODUCT DEFINITION
Executive Product Thesis and Success Narrative
Vyuha is an Agency Integrity Layer embedded in a participating UPI/PSP/banking application and connected to institution-side fraud intelligence. It detects the contextual trajectory that precedes coercion-driven authorised payments, estimates a latent “coercion belief state,” and selects the minimum intervention likely to restore independent decision-making before payment authorization. Digital-arrest scams are the flagship demonstration archetype, but the product category is broader: live-guided authorised payment fraud, including impersonation, fake support, remote-access, emergency, investment and other synchronous coercion patterns.

Why the product exists
Conventional controls are strongest when the attacker steals credentials, impersonates the user, compromises a device, or creates a structurally suspicious beneficiary. A coercion scam is adversarial in a different way: the legitimate account holder may use the legitimate device, open the legitimate payment application, enter the correct PIN, and intentionally press Pay. The transaction can therefore look authenticated while the human decision has been captured. The submitted script correctly identifies the missing temporal surface: the fraud is often created in the interaction trajectory before the transaction, not in the authorization credential itself. [SRC]
North-star outcome
Prevent or materially delay a high-risk coercion-driven payment while adding effectively no perceptible friction to normal low-risk payments. The user must understand why Vyuha intervened, retain a safe path to continue a legitimate transaction, and be able to invoke independent verification without revealing unnecessary private information.


PROBLEM SPACE
Real-World Threat Model: Coercion-Driven Authorised Fraud
India’s financial cyber-fraud burden is sufficiently large that prevention before fund transfer has clear economic and social value. Government figures reported for 2025 show ₹22,495 crore in reported cyber-fraud losses; CFCFRMS figures separately show 24,02,579 financial-fraud complaints on NCRP for 2025 and more than ₹8,189 crore saved through the reporting/management system by 31 December 2025. The Department of Telecommunications’ Financial Fraud Risk Indicator (FRI), launched in May 2025, had by August 2026 reportedly helped prevent more than ₹5,000 crore of suspected fraud transactions across a growing institutional ecosystem. [EXT; Refs. 14–16] These figures do not prove Vyuha’s efficacy; they demonstrate that upstream cross-ecosystem risk signals can have large preventive value.
Threat archetype
• Attacker establishes authority, urgency or fear through a call/video/remote-control channel.
• Victim is isolated from alternative advice and is instructed to keep the communication channel active.
• The attacker gradually obtains compliance without necessarily stealing the victim’s banking credentials.
• Victim opens a genuine payment application and begins a transfer to an instructed beneficiary.
• The victim may truthfully pass device binding, OTP/PIN and account authentication because the victim is the person authorising the transfer.
• After payment, recovery becomes difficult; post-fraud investigation, freezing and chargeback/dispute processes are reactive.
Out of scope
Vyuha is not a replacement for AML, transaction monitoring, malware protection, SIM-swap detection, FRI, device attestation, KYC, beneficiary cooling periods, bank authentication, law-enforcement reporting or UPI network controls. It is a complementary payer-side behavioural context and adaptive intervention layer.


REQUIREMENTS
Product Scope, Non-Goals and Requirement Priorities
The MVP is intentionally narrow at the point of authorization but deep in reasoning. It does not attempt to monitor the user continuously. The host application invokes Vyuha for a protected transaction session, Vyuha collects a bounded context snapshot, updates local risk state, optionally consumes a signed institutional graph-risk token, and returns a decision object that the host renders natively.

Non-goals
• Recording or understanding private calls as a default fraud signal.
• Reading arbitrary WhatsApp/Telegram/SMS content to classify scams.
• Claiming to terminate any third-party call across Android versions; the product instead requests closure/remediation through host/platform-supported paths.
• Executing online reinforcement-learning exploration on real victims.
• Guaranteeing legal compliance solely through architecture; legal/compliance approval remains an institutional process.
• Claiming “0% false positives,” “zero battery drain,” or any latency/accuracy number not produced by the documented benchmark harness.


USERS
Personas, Stakeholders and Jobs-to-Be-Done

Primary jobs-to-be-done
• When I am making a normal payment, let me finish without security theatre.
• When I am being coached by someone during a high-risk payment, create enough separation for me to reassess independently.
• When the system is uncertain, ask the least intrusive question that can safely reduce uncertainty.
• When a recipient is linked to suspicious network behaviour, combine that evidence with my current context instead of treating either signal as sufficient alone.
• When I need a trusted person, let me choose who is contacted and what they see.
• When a bank analyst reviews an event, provide a machine-readable trace of signals, versions, risk contributions and action outcomes.
Human factors requirement
Warnings must not shame the user or imply incompetence. Scammers exploit authority and urgency; therefore Vyuha should reduce urgency rather than add more. Strong interventions use short declarative statements, a visible cooling period, and a safe explanation of why banks/police do not require a user to remain under another person’s direction while transferring money. The UI should avoid disclosing its exact detection thresholds so attackers cannot easily script around them.

USER JOURNEYS
End-to-End Flows: Benign, Ambiguous and Coercion-High


Figure 2. Runtime decision flow. “Local first” means the edge plane can decide without waiting for a remote model.
User-control contract
The SDK returns recommended risk and action, while the participating host owns final transaction enforcement according to regulated policy. For the prototype, the host simulator implements pass, micro-inquiry, reflection, isolation-break, trusted verification and cancel/continue. For production, an institution may map Vyuha actions to its own step-up authentication, cooling period, fraud desk or transaction hold mechanisms. The SDK must never spoof a bank/police authority identity or present itself as law enforcement.

SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE
Three-Plane Architecture and Trust Boundaries

Figure 3. Vyuha 2.0 architecture. Only the first plane is on-device; graph intelligence remains where relationship data exists.
Plane A — Edge Agency Runtime
A lightweight SDK/library embedded in a participating banking/UPI app. It receives host transaction context, coarse device/session signals, local behavioural baseline features and optional graph-risk tokens. It executes the sparse Risk-MoE, belief-state update, uncertainty gate and intervention policy locally. The production runtime should be portable to ExecuTorch/LiteRT or an equivalent signed on-device runtime. [EXT; Refs. 27–28]
Plane B — Institutional Graph Intelligence
A bank/PSP-side service maintains a dynamic heterogeneous graph over entities available to that institution. The ST-GNN produces beneficiary/network risk and compact structural explanations. The edge device receives only a signed, short-lived risk token rather than the graph itself. This corrects the original “local ST-GNN” claim: a handset generally lacks the cross-account topology required for mule-ring inference.
Plane C — Policy, Simulation and Learning
Offline training uses labelled/synthetic interaction trajectories, graph snapshots and a multi-agent scam digital twin. A conservative contextual bandit learns among a fixed action catalogue, with safety constraints and no live exploratory weakening of controls. Production outcomes are logged for later offline evaluation and retraining after governance approval. [EXT; Refs. 23–24]


OFFLINE-FIRST
Connectivity Model, Degradation Logic and Local State
“Offline-first” in Vyuha means that the safety-critical payer-side inference path has no dependency on cloud inference. It does not mean that a national mule graph can be reconstructed on a phone. Online institutional intelligence is an augmentation layer. This distinction is essential for both privacy and feasibility.

Local state
• Session state: transaction ID, start time, recipient pseudonymous identifier, amount bucket, payment rail, current OODA phase.
• Local baseline: quantiles/embeddings for typical amount, beneficiary novelty, time-of-day and recent payment patterns; production design should minimise retention and use host-provided features where possible.
• Belief state: compact scalar/vector summarising current probability/confidence of coercion and missing-data masks.
• Model/config manifest: signed version, hash, calibration set version, policy version, minimum supported host SDK.
• Optional cache: signed recipient risk token with TTL, issuer and nonce binding; never a raw graph extract.


EDGE SENSING
Signal Interface and Privacy-Minimal Feature Engineering
The edge model is only as defensible as its signal contract. Vyuha should request signals that are technically obtainable inside the host application, interpretable, minimally invasive and useful in combination. It should avoid covert cross-app surveillance.

Signals explicitly excluded from core MVP
• raw call audio
• speech transcription
• screen pixels
• camera feed
• keystroke content
• continuous GPS trail
• arbitrary message contents
• full contact book export
Foreground-app detection
Because the intended deployment is inside the participating UPI/bank app, Vyuha already knows when its own protected transaction flow is active. Querying other apps through UsageStatsManager generally requires usage-access permission and explicit device settings; this is not necessary for the core product and should not be a default dependency. [EXT; Ref. 11]

EDGE ML
Sparse Risk-MoE: Conditional Computation Without Buzzword Abuse
The original deck uses a “MoE router” to bypass most normal traffic. The implementation should make that term concrete. The Vyuha Risk-MoE is a small conditional ensemble in which a gate activates only the experts relevant to the current risk context. Sparse gating is motivated by mixture-of-experts work on conditional computation, but Vyuha applies the principle to tiny interpretable risk experts rather than giant language-model capacity. [EXT; Ref. 18]

Gate design
A very small gate g(x) produces expert activation weights. For the MVP, use top-k = 2 or 3 experts plus a mandatory cheap baseline expert. The gate must expose which experts activated and their weights. Conditional execution may reduce average compute, but the project must benchmark this rather than claim “zero overhead.”
Proposed local output
`LocalRisk = {score, expert_scores, gate_weights, confidence, missing_mask, ood_score, model_version}`. The score is not the final decision. It becomes evidence for the belief-state update and policy layer.


SEQUENTIAL INFERENCE
Coercion Belief State: POMDP-Inspired Risk Accumulation
Coercion is latent: the system cannot directly observe whether a person is psychologically controlled. It observes noisy proxies over time. Therefore Vyuha models a short-lived belief state rather than issuing a one-shot classifier verdict. This is conceptually aligned with partially observable decision processes, where actions are chosen from a belief over hidden state. [EXT; Ref. 17]

Belief state contents

Update rule
For the MVP, implement a small GRU/TCN or Bayesian/logit updater over typed event embeddings. The product abstraction is more important than pretending to have an exact psychological model: each new event updates belief, and no isolated event is labelled “scam.” A sequence such as communication-active → new beneficiary → large deviation → capture/control risk → instructed-response pattern should accumulate risk more strongly than any one feature alone.
Why this matters in Q&A
The belief-state framing explains why Vyuha is not a rules engine and why temporal ML exists in the architecture. It also supports adaptive questioning: an intervention creates a new observation, so friction can deliberately reduce uncertainty. That makes the OODA loop functional rather than decorative.

GRAPH INTELLIGENCE
Heterogeneous Spatio-Temporal GNN: Data Model and Rationale

Figure 4. Example heterogeneous temporal graph. Production node/edge availability varies by institution and policy.
Why heterogeneous
Fraud networks are not naturally a single-type account-to-account graph. Risk can emerge from relationships among accounts, VPAs, devices, phones, merchants, IP/network indicators and complaint entities. Heterogeneous graph models use relation/type-specific parameters; HGT is a published architecture for typed heterogeneous graphs and includes temporal encoding ideas. [EXT; Ref. 19]
Why temporal
Mule behaviour is dynamic: fan-in/fan-out bursts, newly activated accounts, short dwell time and rapidly changing neighborhood patterns are temporal phenomena. TGN and TGAT are research foundations for event-time graph learning and inductive temporal representations. [EXT; Refs. 20–21]
Why fraud-specific robustness
Fraudsters camouflage by interacting with benign nodes and changing relations. CARE-GNN demonstrates relation-aware and camouflage-resistant design ideas for fraud graphs. Vyuha does not claim to reproduce CARE-GNN exactly; it borrows the requirement that neighbor selection and relation aggregation be robust to adversarially benign-looking connectivity. [EXT; Ref. 22]


ST-GNN DETAILS
Graph Construction, Temporal Message Passing and Risk Token
The institution ingests event-time edges into a typed graph. Each event is represented as `(src_type, src_id, relation, dst_type, dst_id, timestamp, edge_features)`. The model must support previously unseen nodes and preserve event order. A practical architecture for the prototype is: typed feature encoders → relation-specific projection/attention → temporal encoding/memory → neighborhood aggregation → beneficiary classifier + topology embedding.

Signed risk token
The graph service returns a compact object such as: `issuer, beneficiary_hash, risk_bucket, calibrated_score, topology_codes[], model_version, issued_at, expires_at, nonce, signature`. The edge SDK verifies signature, freshness and nonce/session binding. This prevents replaying an old “safe” token for a different payment and avoids shipping sensitive graph neighborhoods to the handset.
Loss and sampling
Fraud labels are highly imbalanced and delayed. Use time-based train/validation/test splits, negative sampling that preserves realistic degree distributions, focal/class-weighted objectives where justified, and evaluation by precision-recall rather than raw accuracy. The graph model should be tested against camouflage perturbations: adding benign edges, splitting fan-out across mules, delaying bursts and using new nodes.


DECISION INTELLIGENCE
Conformal / Selective Prediction and the Abstention Contract
A payment-safety model should not be forced to pretend certainty. Vyuha therefore separates risk from confidence. Conformal prediction and selective classification motivate distribution-free or calibrated uncertainty sets and explicit abstention, especially in high-stakes settings. [EXT; Ref. 25] The MVP can implement a simpler split-conformal or calibrated confidence wrapper rather than an academically maximal method.

Calibration requirements
• Calibration split must be temporally separated from training data where possible.
• Report reliability diagram / expected calibration error and classwise precision-recall.
• Thresholds are policy artifacts, versioned separately from model weights.
• Threshold changes require evaluation on benign-friction metrics as well as fraud recall.
• Out-of-distribution testing includes unseen scam scripts, unseen recipients, missing-signal combinations and accessibility/device variants.
Product reason
The ByteBuilt fraud theme explicitly values reducing losses without over-flagging genuine users. [SRC] Uncertainty-aware abstention gives Vyuha a technical answer to that judging criterion: the system is designed to know when evidence is insufficient, and the policy can choose a low-cost reversible action instead of pretending the model is infallible.

ADAPTIVE INTERVENTION
Safety-Constrained Contextual Bandit and Offline RL
The action-selection problem is sequential and personalised: the same warning is not optimal for every context, but live exploration in financial safety is unacceptable. Vyuha therefore uses a constrained contextual bandit / offline-RL workflow. Conservative contextual bandit research explicitly studies algorithms constrained to perform no worse than a baseline policy, while offline RL studies policy learning from previously collected data without online exploration. [EXT; Refs. 23–24]

Context vector
Bandit context includes belief score, confidence, active expert scores, amount-loss bucket, payee novelty, access-risk state, graph-risk bucket, prior action, prior user response, accessibility flags and time remaining before authorization. Sensitive raw content is excluded.
Reward design
Use an offline composite reward that strongly penalises a completed known-fraud payment, moderately penalises unnecessary friction on benign sessions, rewards safe cancellation/verification in high-risk sessions, and penalises repeated/confusing interventions. Policy training occurs in simulation and logged datasets; production policy ships as frozen parameters plus hard safety constraints.


AGENTIC RESEARCH
Multi-Agent Scam Digital Twin for Adversarial Policy Training
The most novel agentic component belongs in training, not in the critical transaction path. Vyuha’s Scam Digital Twin is a multi-agent simulation environment that generates adaptive coercion trajectories and tests defensive interventions before they are exposed to users. This lets the project use agentic systems for a problem they are uniquely suited to: red-team adaptation and counterfactual scenario generation.

Scenario grammar
Scenario generation should be structured, not free-form roleplay. Define scam-stage variables such as contact → authority establishment → isolation → remote-control request → beneficiary instruction → payment initiation → intervention → adaptation → outcome. LLM agents may generate linguistic variety offline, but the runtime model trains on structured event traces and labels rather than depending on generated text semantics.
Red-team experiments
• Scammer instructs victim to answer “No” to a direct coercion question.
• Scammer switches from cellular call to VoIP or another device.
• Scammer uses a low-risk new mule with no graph history.
• Scammer splits one large payment into multiple smaller payments.
• Scammer asks user to disable safety tools or accessibility protections.
• Benign comparator: legitimate family member guides an older user through a normal payment.


AGENTIC RUNTIME
OODA Orchestration and Deterministic Multi-Agent Boundaries
The deck’s OODA framing can remain, but the runtime “agents” should be typed services/state machines rather than several LLMs debating a payment. This gives the system agentic decomposition without nondeterministic chatter on the safety-critical path.

State-machine rules
• Every session has a globally unique transaction/session nonce.
• The Policy Agent can choose only actions in the institution-approved catalogue.
• The host can veto or strengthen an action according to institutional policy, but must log the override reason.
• A completed high-friction action cannot loop indefinitely; maximum intervention count and timeout are explicit.
• Model/policy version is immutable within one protected transaction session.
• Generative explanations, if used, are post-decision and templated/guarded; the reason code is the source of truth.
Why LangGraph is optional
LangGraph can orchestrate the prototype and visualize state transitions, but it is an implementation framework, not the innovation. Production may replace it with Kotlin/C++ state machines or a service workflow. The PRD therefore specifies typed states and contracts independently of orchestration library.

INTERVENTIONS
Isolation Break Protocol and Cognitive Friction Design
The signature intervention is not “a smarter warning.” It is a controlled break in the synchronous scammer–victim feedback loop. The attacker’s advantage is often continuous authority and coaching. Vyuha should deliberately create a short, safe interval in which the user can act without external direction.

Platform-backed isolation
Android Play Integrity can report whether apps are running that could capture the screen, display overlays or control the device. Current remediation dialogs include mechanisms to prompt closure of unknown or all capture/control risks. [EXT; Refs. 7–9] This is materially stronger and more privacy-preserving than attempting to enumerate every installed app or draw an unsafe overlay above a banking UI.
Copy requirements
• Do not say “You are being scammed” when the model is uncertain.
• Do say what the system observed at a high level: “A screen-control risk is active during this payment.”
• Do not expose exact model thresholds, graph features or rule combinations.
• Always provide a legitimate recovery path: close risky access, verify independently, or contact the bank.
• Use plain language and regional-language-ready string resources; voice output is optional and must never read sensitive payment details aloud by default.

TRUSTED VERIFICATION
Safety Circle, Bank Escalation and Consent Model
The original “family emergency ping” becomes a user-configurable Safety Circle. Family is not universally safe, available or appropriate. The product must support a trusted friend, caregiver, sibling, spouse, or no personal contact, with a bank-fraud-desk fallback where available.

Consent and data minimization
The DPDP Act and Rules establish obligations around lawful processing, notice, purpose limitation and security safeguards; this PRD does not claim automatic compliance. [EXT; Refs. 12–13] Product requirements are therefore conservative: Vyuha must expose a host-readable data inventory, purpose strings, retention settings, consent/notice hooks where consent is the chosen basis, and deletion/export interfaces for data the host determines is personal data under applicable law.


INTERFACES
Android SDK / Host Contract
The SDK API is designed around a protected payment session. Names below are normative conceptual interfaces; implementation language may be Kotlin/Java with a native C++ inference core.

Core data classes
`RiskAssessment { riskScore, riskTier, confidence, abstain, localExpertScores, graphRisk?, missingMask, reasonCodes[], modelVersion, policyVersion, timings }`
`InterventionDecision { actionId, severity, copyKey, remediationType?, coolingMs?, escalationAllowed, hostEnforcementHint, reasonCodes[] }`
`DecisionTrace { sessionNonce, eventHashes[], assessmentHash, actionHistory[], outcomeCode, model/policy versions, latencyBreakdown }`
Host integration rules
• The host renders all payment UI natively; Vyuha does not require SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW for core flows.
• The host must call `startSession` before final authorization and must not reuse a session nonce across transactions.
• The host must support a fail-open/fail-safe policy configured by the institution; SDK exceptions cannot silently masquerade as PASS.
• Personally identifying fields should be tokenized/pseudonymized before entering the local ML feature vector where not needed in raw form.

INTERFACES
Institutional Graph Service, Event Schemas and Signed Tokens
The institutional interface can be REST or gRPC; the logical contract is stable. The graph service is not queried by beneficiary raw account number from an untrusted client. The participating host/backend should broker requests, bind them to the transaction and return a signed token to the edge SDK.

Example event envelope
`{event_id, ts, src_type, src_id_token, relation, dst_type, dst_id_token, amount_bucket, channel, institution_id, label_state, provenance}``
Retention and lineage
• Every training example must retain provenance to source table/event class and label timestamp so post-event labels do not leak into earlier predictions.
• Graph snapshots and train/test splits are time-versioned to prevent future-edge leakage.
• Outcome labels have states: UNKNOWN, USER_CANCELLED, VERIFIED_SAFE, SUSPECTED_FRAUD, CONFIRMED_FRAUD; model training uses only approved label states.
• Model registry records graph schema version, feature code hash, sampling configuration, training date range and calibration date range.

SECURITY
Abuse Cases, Adversarial ML and Defensive Controls

Adversarial ML test suite
• Feature suppression: remove one signal family and verify graceful degradation.
• Feature contradiction: graph high / local low and graph low / local high.
• Temporal perturbation: reorder benign events; delay/coalesce scam events.
• Camouflage: add benign graph neighbors and spread fan-out across accounts.
• Threshold probing: repeated borderline sessions should not reveal exact policy cutoffs.
• Model extraction resistance: SDK exposes reason codes, not raw logits for every internal expert to untrusted callers.

ML LIFECYCLE
Datasets, Training, Versioning and Research Protocol
A technically ambitious architecture becomes credible only when data provenance and evaluation are explicit. The hackathon prototype can use synthetic graph and interaction data, but every demo must label synthetic evidence as synthetic. Production efficacy claims require institution-approved historical/pilot data.

Training pipeline
1. Freeze schema and label definition; generate/extract time-versioned dataset.
2. Train local experts and graph model independently; measure component metrics.
3. Calibrate probabilities / conformal scores on held-out time slice.
4. Generate policy trajectories from digital twin and logged outcomes; train conservative action policy offline.
5. Run integrated replay with exact runtime feature transformations.
6. Export quantized edge bundle; benchmark on actual Android device through the same SDK interface.
7. Register hashes/versions; only then promote to pilot/shadow environment.
Future federated learning
Federated averaging is a research-backed pattern for training across decentralised data without centralising raw records. [EXT; Ref. 26] A future cross-institution Vyuha could federate selected model updates, but regulatory, competitive, poisoning and non-IID issues make this a roadmap item, not an MVP dependency.

EVALUATION
Benchmark Matrix, Metrics and Claim Gate

Figure 6. Evaluation ladder. Hackathon evidence can satisfy early stages; production claims require later stages.

Claim gate
A number appears in the pitch only if the corresponding benchmark artifact exists. For example, “sub-50 ms” requires measured p95 local inference on named hardware and software build; “95% fast path” requires a benign evaluation distribution and fast-path proportion; “low false positives” requires a defined negative set and confidence interval. Until then, write “target” or “design objective.”

OBSERVABILITY & RELIABILITY
SLOs, Logs, Model Monitoring and Failure Recovery

SLOs and recovery
• [TARGET] SDK availability >=99.95% within a healthy host process; crash-free protected sessions >=99.99% after production hardening.
• Risk service timeout is bounded (e.g., 120 ms budget in the host backend); timeout produces UNKNOWN_GRAPH, not PASS.
• Bundle update is atomic: download → signature/hash verify → compatibility check → staged activation → health monitor → rollback on failure.
• A model/policy can be remotely disabled by signed kill-switch configuration while retaining a deterministic baseline safety policy.
• Time synchronisation errors beyond tolerance invalidate short-lived risk tokens and fall back to local mode.
• All high-friction decisions expose a stable reason code for support and audit; internal neural activations are not presented as explanations.
Model monitoring
Monitor feature missingness, score distributions, calibration drift, intervention rate, graph token age, action outcomes and subgroup/accessibility regressions. Drift alerts trigger investigation, not automatic online retraining. A new model is promoted only through the lifecycle on page 25.

HACKATHON EXECUTION
24-Hour Vertical Slice, Team Split and Demo Acceptance
The ByteBuilt rulebook requires the final teams to build/refine the prototype during the 24-hour finale and submit a working model, source code, presentation and documentation. It also states that projects must be original and existing projects cannot be submitted. [SRC] The team should therefore treat this PRD, research, diagrams and planning as preparation, while competition-specific implementation follows organiser rules and any clarification received at briefing.

Must-demo acceptance sequence
1. Benign payment executes with no Vyuha UI; trace shows fast path and measured local latency.
2. High-risk scenario starts with active communication/access-risk + anomalous amount/new beneficiary.
3. Graph service scores a suspicious beneficiary from the live synthetic temporal graph and returns signed token.
4. Belief state rises across events; UI can display an engineering/debug trace for judges without exposing this to end users.
5. Policy selects isolation-break / close-risk / cooling action; user can verify or cancel.
6. Dashboard shows model versions, reason codes, graph motif and measured benchmark values, clearly marked synthetic where applicable.
